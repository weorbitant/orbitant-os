# Orbitant — Narrative Activation Framework

> **Usage instructions:** This document defines *how* Orbitant's narrative is activated
> across all touchpoints. Use it as the operational layer on top of `orbitant-narrative.md`.
> When generating content, proposals, job descriptions, or any external communication,
> apply the vocabulary system, tone rules, and content pillars defined here.
> Do not use generic tech consultancy language — use the Orbit Language system below.

---

## 1. The Narrative Architecture

The narrative is not a marketing document. It is the strategic core that directs brand, culture, and business decisions. Narrative sits at the centre — not inside marketing, not inside sales, not inside HR. It informs all of them.

The CEO is the Chief Narrative Officer. The narrative is owned by leadership, not delegated to marketing. Every strategic decision should be testable against: *does this move us and our clients toward a higher orbit?*

---

## 2. Vocabulary System — The Orbit Language

**This is the most important section for content generation.** Consistent language creates consistent thinking. These terms must be used in all internal and external communications. Never use the legacy terms in the right column.

| Concept | Orbit Language (USE THIS) | Replaces (NEVER USE) |
|---|---|---|
| Client's current state | **Current orbit** | "As-is", "legacy state" |
| What holds them back | **Gravity** | "Blockers", "friction", "debt" |
| The transformation goal | **New orbit / higher orbit** | "Target state", "digital transformation" |
| Orbitant's role | **Thrust** | "Our services", "our offering" |
| Falling behind | **Orbital decay** | "Losing competitiveness" |
| The AI-driven shift | **Gravitational shift** | "Market disruption", "AI revolution" |
| Innovation scouting | **Orbiting** | "R&D", "exploration" |
| Achieving transformation | **Escape velocity** | "Go-live", "maturity" |
| Companies that made it | **In orbit** | "Success stories", "case studies" |

### Where to apply the Orbit Language

- **Proposals and presales**: frame every engagement as "current orbit → new orbit"
- **Internal updates**: report progress in orbit terms ("client X achieved escape velocity on their CI/CD pipeline")
- **Marketing content**: LinkedIn, blog, talks — use the metaphor as a consistent lens
- **Hiring**: "we're looking for people who provide thrust"

## 4. Sales and Presales Narrative

The biggest shift: stop selling services, start enrolling clients into the worldview.

### The presales conversation structure

1. **The gravitational shift** — "AI has changed the rules. Code is commoditising. The gap between well-engineered and poorly-engineered companies is becoming catastrophic, not just inconvenient."
2. **The diagnosis** — "Let's look at your current orbit. Where are your foundations strong? Where is gravity pulling you down?"
3. **The orbit map** — "Here's where you need to be in 12–18 months. Here's what escape velocity looks like for your specific context."
4. **The thrust** — "Here's how we get you there. Engineering + innovation + business understanding, working as one force."
5. **The proof** — "Here's a company that was in a similar orbit. Here's where they are now."

### Proposal structure

Every proposal must contain these five sections:

- **Current Orbit Assessment**: where the client is today
- **Gravitational Forces**: what's pulling them down or shifting the landscape
- **Target Orbit**: where they need to be, tied to business outcomes
- **Thrust Plan**: the engineering, innovation, and strategic work required
- **Escape Velocity Metrics**: how we measure success

### Pricing narrative

"We don't sell hours. We provide the thrust to reach your next orbit. The investment reflects the distance between where you are and where you need to be."

Discovery work is paid. This filters for serious companies and positions Orbitant as a strategic partner, not a vendor.

## 5. Hiring Narrative

The narrative should be the first thing a candidate encounters and the primary filter for cultural fit.

### Job description opening (use this or adapt it)

> *"We believe we're living through humanity's greatest acceleration. AI is reshaping every industry, and the companies that will thrive are the ones with the engineering discipline, innovation mindset, and business understanding to harness it. Orbitant exists to be the thrust that gets them there. We're looking for engineers who don't just write code — they make the decisions that determine what to build and why."*

### Interview filter questions

- "What technology shift in the last 2 years do you think most companies are underestimating?"
- "Tell me about a time you pushed back on a technical decision because it didn't serve the business outcome."
- "What does engineering excellence mean when AI can write most of the code?"

### Onboarding sequence

- Day 1: read the founding narrative. Discuss it with a founder.
- Week 1: attend a knowledge sharing session.
- Month 1: present a learning or insight to the team.

## 6. Content Strategy

GuideSmiths' biggest regret: no external community, no narrative, no perceived market leadership. Orbitant's content strategy is the direct correction of that failure.

### Content pillars

| Pillar | Theme | Formats |
|---|---|---|
| **The Acceleration** | Civilisational inflection point, exponential progress, what's changing | LinkedIn posts, keynotes, podcast appearances (Felipe + founders) |
| **Engineering in the AI Era** | Why engineering matters more not less; the shift from coding to judgment | Technical blog posts, conference talks, dev community engagement |
| **Orbit Stories** | Client transformations told through the orbit metaphor | Case studies, video testimonials, presales collateral |
| **From the Frontier** | What Orbitant is discovering through its innovation scouting | Tech Radar updates, emerging tech analysis, applied AI insights |
| **Lessons from the Journey** | GuideSmiths → Orbitant story, honest lessons, building in public | Founder content, team stories, behind-the-scenes |

### Publishing cadence

- **Weekly**: 1–2 LinkedIn posts from founders (worldview, not promotional)
- **Biweekly**: 1 technical article or insight from the team
- **Monthly**: 1 orbit story (case study) or frontier report
- **Quarterly**: 1 Tech Radar update published externally

### Tone rules

- **Confident, not arrogant** — conviction comes from experience, not ego
- **Grounded in experience** — the GuideSmiths story gives credibility; use it
- **Forward-looking but honest** — acknowledge uncertainty, don't oversell
- **Technical depth when appropriate, strategic clarity always**
- **Never motivational or guru-like** — factual, opinionated, useful
- **Never generic** — avoid "digital transformation", "innovation ecosystem", "leveraging AI"

## 7. Measuring Narrative Health

A narrative that doesn't change behaviour is just a document. These signals indicate whether the narrative is working:

| Signal | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Clients use orbit language back to you | The metaphor is landing |
| Candidates mention the narrative in interviews | Hiring magnetism is working |
| Team uses orbit terms naturally in standups | Internal adoption is real |
| Inbound mentions the worldview | Content strategy is projecting |
| Proposals win on vision, not just price | Narrative is creating differentiation |
| GuideSmiths story resonates in sales | Origin story adds credibility |

### Quarterly narrative review questions

- Is the worldview still sharp or has it softened into generic language?
- Are we living the values or just listing them?
- Are we actually orbiting (scouting the frontier) or just delivering?
- Does our external presence reflect the narrative or lag behind it?

---

## 8. Narrative Governance

**Owner**: CEO (Chief Narrative Officer by default)

**Core team**: CEO + 1 from Brand, 1 from Culture/HR, 1 from Engineering leadership

**Cadence**: Quarterly narrative alignment — review how the narrative is manifesting across brand, culture, sales, and hiring. Adjust language, not beliefs. If the beliefs change, it's a founding-level conversation.

**The orbit test**: Every new initiative, partnership, service offering, or major hire must pass this test — *does this help us or our clients reach a higher orbit?* If not, it doesn't belong.
