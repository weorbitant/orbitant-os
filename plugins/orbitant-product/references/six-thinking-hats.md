# Six Thinking Hats

**Origin.** Dr. Edward de Bono, 1985 (*Six Thinking Hats*).

## Core idea

A parallel-thinking technique: instead of debating everything at once (where opinions
dominate), the group — or a single person — adopts **one "hat" (perspective) at a time**,
so everyone looks at the problem from the same angle simultaneously. This separates facts
from emotions, and criticism from creativity.

## The six hats

| Hat | Perspective |
|---|---|
| ⚪ **White** | Data, facts, available information (and what's missing) |
| 🔴 **Red** | Emotions, intuition, gut feelings (no need to justify) |
| ⚫ **Black** | Caution, risks, what could fail |
| 🟡 **Yellow** | Optimism, benefits, value, why it would work |
| 🟢 **Green** | Creativity, alternatives, new ideas |
| 🔵 **Blue** | Metacognition: process control, "are we on the right track?" |

## How it's applied in the guide

It appears among the **advanced elicitation techniques**: when the PM gets stuck or takes
something as obvious, walking the six angles surfaces aspects a single lens leaves out
(especially black — risks — and green — alternatives).
