# The Mom Test

**Origin.** Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (2013). How to talk to users without getting
false positives.

## Core idea

People lie out of politeness: if you show them your idea with enthusiasm, they'll tend to
tell you what you want to hear. The fix isn't to ask your mom better questions, but to
**not ask anyone whether your idea is good** — that's a poorly framed question that always
yields biased feedback.

## The three rules

1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Explore their real experiences and problems
   instead of pitching your solution.
2. **Ask about specifics in the past**, not opinions or hypotheticals about the future
   ("would you…?", "would you like…?" give unreliable answers).
3. **Talk less and listen more.**

## How it's applied in the guide

- **Rule 4 (don't accept vague answers)** and the general spirit of the interrogation: the
  guide's questions seek facts and concrete decisions ("is it allowed? what exactly should
  happen?"), not validation or generic opinions.
- It's a warning for the interrogator: don't lead the answer or settle for a complacent
  "yes".
