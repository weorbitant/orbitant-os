# Assumption Mapping

**Origin.** David J. Bland and Alexander Osterwalder (*Testing Business Ideas*, 2019);
roots in *Lean Startup* (the "riskiest assumption").

## Core idea

Every proposal rests on **assumptions** taken as true without evidence. Mapping makes them
explicit and prioritizes them on a 2×2 matrix:

- **Importance** axis: if this assumption is false, does the idea collapse? (important ↔
  trivial)
- **Evidence** axis: how much do we actually know? (unknown ↔ known)

The **important and unknown** assumptions are the **riskiest**: they must be tested first,
before investing in building.

They usually fall into three families: **desirability** (do they want it?), **viability**
(does it make sense for the business?), and **feasibility** (can it be done?).

## How it's applied in the guide

- It appears as the **"assumption stress-test"** among the elicitation techniques: take
  each stated assumption and look for a case that breaks it.
- **Block 8 and block 11**: "what are you taking for granted that might not be true?"
  surfaces assumptions so they can be flagged as a risk or as `[TO CLARIFY]`.
