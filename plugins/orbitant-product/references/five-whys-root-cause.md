# The 5 Whys / Root cause analysis

**Origin.** Sakichi Toyoda and the Toyota Production System (*lean manufacturing*); later
adopted by *Lean Startup* and continuous improvement.

## Core idea

Faced with a problem, ask **"why?"** repeatedly (typically about five times) to cut through
the symptoms and reach the **root cause**. Each answer becomes the basis for the next
question.

Five is a rule of thumb, not a law: you stop when you reach a cause you can act on. The
goal is to avoid fixing a symptom while leaving the cause that generates it untouched.

## How it's applied in the guide

- Block **2 (problem to solve)**: the question "is this the problem or a symptom?" invokes
  the 5 Whys to tell the real pain from the apparent one.
- Keeps the *Problem brief* from documenting a symptom as if it were the problem.

## Example

> "Users don't complete signup." → why? → "they drop off at step 3" → why? → "they don't
> have a piece of data we ask for at hand" → why? → "we ask for it before it's needed"… →
> actionable root cause.

## Source
